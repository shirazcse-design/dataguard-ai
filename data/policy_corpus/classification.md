# Data Classification Standard

## 1. Purpose and scope

This standard defines how Harbourline Group classifies information and the minimum handling
requirements for each classification level. It applies to all employees, contractors and third
parties who create, store, process or transmit Harbourline information, in any format.

## 2. Classification levels

### 2.1 Public

Public information has been approved for release outside the company, such as published marketing
material, press releases and public product documentation. No special handling is required.

### 2.2 Internal

Internal information is intended for use inside Harbourline and is not approved for public
release, such as internal announcements, organisation charts and non-sensitive project documents.
Unauthorised disclosure would cause limited harm.

### 2.3 Confidential

Confidential information would cause significant harm if disclosed. It includes customer personal
information, customer financial information, non-public financial results, employee records and
proprietary source code. Access is limited to people with a business need to know.

### 2.4 Restricted

Restricted information would cause severe harm if disclosed. It includes protected health
information (PHI), payment card data, authentication credentials and secrets, and information
about planned mergers and acquisitions. Restricted information is the highest classification level.

## 3. Handling requirements

### 3.1 Labelling

All documents classified Confidential or Restricted must carry a visible classification label. The
data owner is responsible for applying the correct label when the document is created.

### 3.2 Storage

Confidential and Restricted information may only be stored in company-managed systems approved by
Information Security. It must never be stored on personal devices or in personal accounts,
including personal email and personal cloud-storage accounts.

### 3.3 Transmission

Confidential information sent outside the company must be encrypted in transit (TLS 1.2 or higher,
or an encrypted attachment) and sent only to recipients with a business need. Restricted
information may be sent outside the company only with the data owner's written approval and must
be encrypted.

### 3.4 Printing and disposal

Printed Confidential and Restricted documents must be collected from the printer immediately and
disposed of in secure shredding bins. Electronic media must be securely wiped before reuse.

## 4. Data owners

Every information asset has a data owner, normally the head of the business function that created
it. The data owner assigns the classification level and approves access and sharing requests.
Disputes about classification are escalated to the Chief Information Security Officer.
